# User Guide: Multi-Format Study Notes

## Overview

Your study notes can now be generated in two different formats to match your teaching style and tradition:

- **Christian Sermon Format** - Traditional sermon study notes
- **Jewish Teaching Format** - Torah/rabbinic study notes with commentary

## How to Use

### Step 1: View Your Study Notes

Navigate to any study notes page from your dashboard.

### Step 2: Click "Regenerate Notes"

Look for the blue "Regenerate Notes" button near the top of the page, next to the Copy, Print, and Download buttons.

### Step 3: Choose Your Format

A dialog will appear with two options:

#### Option 1: Christian Sermon Format
- Summary
- Key Points  
- Scriptures
- Application

**Best for:**
- Church sermons
- Bible studies
- Devotionals
- Christian teaching content

#### Option 2: Jewish Teaching Format
- Summary
- Main Text (Parashah/Source)
- Key Points
- Scriptures
- Commentary (Rashi, Talmud, Midrash)
- Ethical Insight (Mussar)
- Discussion Questions (for chavruta)
- Application
- Historical & Linguistic Notes

**Best for:**
- Torah studies
- Rabbinic teachings
- Jewish educational content
- Synagogue teachings

### Step 4: Wait for Processing

After selecting a format:
- You'll see a notification that regeneration has started
- The process takes 1-3 minutes depending on the length of your content
- The page will automatically refresh when complete

### Step 5: Explore Your New Format

Once regeneration is complete, you'll see:
- Updated tabs based on your chosen format
- New sections specific to that format
- All content reorganized to match the format structure

## What's Different Between Formats?

### Christian Format Sections

1. **Summary** - Overview of the sermon's main message
2. **Key Points** - Main takeaways numbered and organized
3. **Scriptures** - Bible references with full verse text
4. **Application** - Practical ways to apply the message
5. **Discussion Questions** - For group study or personal reflection

### Jewish Format Sections

Everything from Christian format, PLUS:

1. **Main Text (Parashah)** - The primary Torah portion, verse, or rabbinic quote that was discussed
   - Automatically identified from the teaching
   - Displayed prominently in its own tab

2. **Commentary Layer** - Rabbinic insights and commentary
   - Sources like "Rashi says..." or "Talmud Bavli discusses..."
   - Multiple commentary sources organized by scholar/text
   - Helps understand different interpretations

3. **Ethical Insight (Mussar)** - Moral and life reflection
   - Focused on character development
   - Practical wisdom for daily living
   - Grounded in Jewish ethical tradition

4. **Historical & Linguistic Notes** - Deeper context
   - Etymology of Hebrew terms
   - Historical background
   - Cultural context
   - Linguistic insights

5. **Discussion Questions** - Designed for chavruta (paired study)
   - Questions that promote dialogue
   - Encourage deeper exploration
   - Suitable for group learning

## Tips for Best Results

### Choosing the Right Format

**Use Christian Format when:**
- The content is a church sermon
- The speaker primarily references the New Testament
- The teaching style is expository or topical
- You want traditional sermon study notes

**Use Jewish Format when:**
- The content is a Torah study or rabbinic teaching
- The speaker references Torah, Talmud, or rabbinic sources
- The teaching includes Hebrew terms or concepts
- You want commentary-style notes

### Can I Switch Back?

Yes! You can regenerate notes as many times as you want:
- Your original transcript is never deleted
- Each regeneration creates fresh notes from the transcript
- Try both formats to see which works better for your content

### What If the Format Doesn't Fit?

The AI does its best to extract relevant information for each format:
- If your Christian sermon is regenerated in Jewish format, it will still work but may have fewer Jewish-specific sections
- If your Torah study is in Christian format, it will focus on the universal themes
- You can always switch to the other format if the first doesn't feel right

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Will I lose my old notes?
**A:** Yes, regeneration replaces the old notes. However, your original transcript is preserved, so you can always regenerate again.

### Q: How long does regeneration take?
**A:** Usually 1-3 minutes, depending on the length of your transcript. Longer teachings may take up to 5 minutes.

### Q: Can I have both formats at once?
**A:** Not currently. Each study note can only be in one format at a time. You can switch between formats as needed.

### Q: What if some sections are empty?
**A:** The AI generates sections based on the content. If your teaching doesn't contain certain elements (like rabbinic commentary), those sections may be minimal or empty.

### Q: Can I edit the generated notes?
**A:** Currently, you can view and copy the notes, but direct editing isn't available. You can copy the content and edit it in your own document.

### Q: Does this work for all languages?
**A:** The AI works best with English content. Hebrew terms and phrases are supported in Jewish format, but the main transcript should be in English.

## Examples

### Example: Christian Format

**Summary:**
"This sermon explores the theme of peace in Philippians 4:6-7, emphasizing how prayer and thanksgiving lead to God's peace that surpasses understanding."

**Key Points:**
1. Anxiety is a universal human experience
2. Prayer is the antidote to worry
3. God's peace guards our hearts and minds
4. Thanksgiving shifts our perspective

**Scriptures:**
- Philippians 4:6-7: "Do not be anxious about anything..."
- Matthew 6:25-34: "Therefore I tell you, do not worry..."

**Application:**
- Practice daily prayer and thanksgiving
- Write down specific worries and pray about them
- Memorize Philippians 4:6-7

### Example: Jewish Format

**Main Text:**
"Parashat Bereshit, Genesis 1:1-2:3 - The Creation narrative"

**Commentary Layer:**
- **Rashi**: "In the beginning - Rabbi Isaac said: The Torah should have begun with 'This month shall be to you...'"
- **Talmud Bavli**: "The world was created with ten utterances. What does this teach us? That the righteous who sustain the world receive great reward..."

**Ethical Insight (Mussar):**
"Just as God rested on the seventh day, we are called to practice Shabbat as a discipline of trust and gratitude, recognizing that our worth is not in our productivity but in our being."

**Historical Notes:**
- **Bereshit**: From the Hebrew root meaning "in the beginning," this word opens the entire Torah and establishes the framework for understanding creation.
- **Shabbat**: Derived from the verb "to cease" or "to rest," representing both cessation from work and active rest in God's presence.
